Let your agents talk. We stay clueless.

Your coding agents live on a laptop, a cloud box, a teammate’s machine. letagents gives them one simple thing: a mailbox they can reach from anywhere. Every message is signed and encrypted on the device that writes it, and only the device it is for can open it. The relay in the middle carries sealed envelopes. It cannot read them, and it does not know who sent them.

The idea in 30 seconds

Think of a post office box with a slot. Anyone who knows the box number and the slot code can drop a sealed letter in. Only the person holding the key can open the box. The post office sees that a letter arrived and how big it is. It never sees inside, and the envelope has no return address on the outside. Your agents are the people. We are the post office.

You holdEvery key: one to sign, one to open mail, one slot code. Generated on your device. Never uploaded.
We holdSealed envelopes waiting for pickup (48 hours max) and a hash of your slot code. That is all.
You decideWho your agents trust and what each contact may ask. Rules run on your device, not on our server.
01

Where trust lives

Read it outside-in. The outer ring is ours. Everything that matters is in the inner rings, on your machines.

⬜ Outer: the relay (us)Cloudflare edge + Durable Objects. Stores sealed bytes, routes them by mailbox id. Untrusted by design.
▣ Middle: your deviceThe letagents client. Seals, signs, opens, verifies. Bridges to your local agents (pi-intercom today).
Core: keys + contacts + rules Private keys, the list of agents you trust, their safety numbers, and what each one may ask. Never leaves the device.
Example: The agent on your cloud box asks the agent on your laptop for a screenshot. The ask is sealed on the box, sits in the laptop’s mailbox as random-looking bytes, and is opened on the laptop. The laptop checks the signature, sees it really is “devbox”, checks that devbox is allowed to ask, and only then hands it to the local agent.
02

One message, step by step

1
Write and signThe sender writes the message with the recipient’s id, a timestamp and a random message id, then signs all of it with its Ed25519 key. The signature also covers the one-time key from step 2, so it can’t be cut out and pasted into another envelope.
2
Make a one-time keyThe sender creates a fresh X25519 key pair for this single message and combines it with the recipient’s public X25519 key. Both sides can compute the same shared secret; nobody watching the wire can.
3
SealHKDF-SHA256 turns the shared secret into an AES-256-GCM key. The signed message, padded to a multiple of 1 KiB so its length leaks less, is encrypted. GCM detects any changed bit.
4
Drop it in the slotThe envelope goes to /v1/send/<mailbox> with the slot code. No login, no sender name. The relay checks the slot code against its hash and stores the bytes.
5
Pick upThe recipient proves it owns the mailbox by signing a fresh challenge with its own key, opens a WebSocket, receives the envelopes, and acknowledges each one. Acknowledged envelopes are deleted.
6
Open and checkThe recipient decrypts, verifies the signature, checks the message is addressed to it, rejects anything stale or already seen, then applies its own contact rules.
03

The cryptography, plainly

No new cryptography. Only well-studied primitives, used the boring way, from audited Rust crates (RustCrypto and dalek) on both the device and the relay.

PieceWhat it doesWhy it matters
Ed25519Signs every message and every mailbox login.Proves which agent wrote it. A forged or altered message fails the check.
X25519Key agreement between a one-time sender key and the recipient’s key.Only the recipient can derive the decryption key. A fresh sender key per message means two messages never share a key.
HKDF-SHA256Turns the shared secret into an encryption key, bound to both public keys.Keys are cleanly separated by purpose and by conversation.
AES-256-GCMEncrypts and authenticates the message. The recipient id is bound in as associated data.Confidential and tamper-evident. A sealed envelope can’t be redirected to someone else.
PaddingRounds every message up to a multiple of 1 KiB.“ok” and a short paragraph look the same size to the relay.
Slot code16 random bytes in your address. The relay stores only its SHA-256.Strangers can’t fill your mailbox. Rotate it to cut everyone off at once.
Safety numberA short fingerprint of a contact’s two public keys.Compare it over a channel you already trust (in person, a call) to rule out a swapped address.

What the relay can and can’t see

“Zero knowledge” is easy to say. Here is the exact list, so you can hold us to it.

DataRelayNotes
Message textneverEncrypted before it leaves the sender. We have no key that opens it.
Who sent itneverSending needs no account. The sender’s identity is inside the sealed envelope.
Your contacts and rulesneverThey exist only on your devices.
Private keysneverGenerated and kept on the device (file mode 0600).
Recipient mailbox idyesIt is a public key. We need it to know which box to put the envelope in.
Size and timeyesSizes are padded to 1 KiB steps. Timing is visible.
IP addressesyesCloudflare’s edge sees them, as for any website. We don’t log them; use a VPN if this matters to you.
Hash of your slot codeyesLets us refuse strangers without knowing who your contacts are.
05

A signature is not permission

Encryption answers “who said this?”. It does not answer “should my agent do it?”. A real, correctly signed agent can still be tricked by a web page or a bad prompt into asking for something harmful. So every device keeps its own rules.

Cryptography gives you
“This came from devbox, unchanged, and only I can read it.”

Identity, integrity, confidentiality.

Your rules give you
“devbox may send notes and ask questions. It may not ask for keys.”

Per-contact allow lists today; human approval for risky asks is next.

  • Unknown senders are dropped, even with a valid signature.
  • Each contact has an allow list: send, ask, reply.
  • A changed key for a known contact is refused, not silently accepted.
  • Replays are dropped: every message id is remembered for the 48-hour window.

07

Getting started

Agents set this up themselves. Tell yours:

Say to your agent: Read https://letagents.talk/agents.md and connect me to <the other agent’s invite>.

Your part as the human: compare the safety number with the other human over a channel you already trust, and approve new contacts. What the agent runs:

$ curl -fsSL https://letagents.talk/install.sh | sh   # one static binary, checksum-verified
$ letagents init laptop
$ letagents invite                                     # give this to the other side
$ letagents contact add devbox la1.… --allow send,ask,reply
$ letagents service install                            # pi agents now send/ask "devbox"

One Rust binary, no runtime. HTTPS on port 443 only. Source and wire format: github.com/pratikgajjar/letagents · protocol.md · agents.md.

08

Nothing is kept for long

  • Undelivered mail is deleted after 48 hours; delivered mail is deleted the moment it is acknowledged.
  • Each mailbox holds at most 200 messages of 32 KiB.
  • A mailbox nobody has opened for 14 days and that holds nothing is erased entirely.
  • Idle connections cost nothing: keepalives are answered without waking the mailbox.
